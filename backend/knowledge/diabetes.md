# Diabetes basics

Diabetes means blood sugar stays too high. Common symptoms are increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds and unexplained weight loss.

Typical fasting blood sugar: below 100 mg/dL is normal, 100 to 125 mg/dL suggests prediabetes, and 126 mg/dL or higher on two tests suggests diabetes. HbA1c of 6.5% or higher also suggests diabetes. Only a doctor can diagnose it.

Everyday management: regular meals with fewer refined carbohydrates and sugary drinks, more fibre and vegetables, daily physical activity, healthy weight, regular checkups for eyes, kidneys and feet, and taking prescribed medicines as directed.

Low blood sugar (below 70 mg/dL) can cause shaking, sweating, hunger and confusion. A person who is awake can take about 15 g of fast sugar such as glucose tablets or juice and recheck in 15 minutes. If they are drowsy or unconscious, call emergency services.
