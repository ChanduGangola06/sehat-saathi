# Fever: general guidance

A fever is usually a body temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher. It is a symptom, most often of an infection, not a disease by itself.

Self-care for adults with a mild fever: rest, drink plenty of fluids, wear light clothing, and monitor the temperature. Many adults use paracetamol according to the package label, but anyone with liver disease, pregnancy, or other conditions should check with a doctor or pharmacist first.

See a doctor soon if a fever lasts more than three days, comes back repeatedly, or is accompanied by a rash, severe headache, stiff neck, or persistent vomiting.

Get urgent care if the fever is 40 °C (104 °F) or higher, if there is confusion, trouble breathing, or seizures, or if the person is an infant under 3 months old with any fever. In regions with dengue or malaria, fever with body pain, rash, or bleeding signs needs a blood test promptly.
